# HP graph-count screen decision

Defer the graph-count 4 to 16 screen in the remaining window. The measured memory headroom permits considering a bounded experiment, but the existing HP receipts do not establish a measured throughput opportunity of at least 5 percent from additional graph admission. The source shows a plausible admission mechanism; the 5–15 percent prediction remains a hypothesis.

## Memory budget

The original graph-4 HP GPU sampler B1 guard minima for GPUs 0 through 7 are 1,707, 1,739, 1,739, 1,739, 1,739, 1,739, 1,739 and 1,739 MiB. The B2 minima are 1,711, 1,743, 1,743, 1,743, 1,743, 1,743, 1,743 and 1,743 MiB. Both pass the original 1 GiB admission condition. The newer descriptive profile reached 1,699 MiB minimum, but its prior long-context history differs from the fresh matched timing arms.

For a single uniform stop reserve that enforces no more than 512 MiB additional peak usage on any GPU relative to B1, use max(512, max(per-GPU B1 minimum) - 512) = 1,227 MiB. Using only the smallest baseline GPU minimum would under-protect GPUs 1 through 7. Relative to B2 the corresponding reserve is 1,231 MiB. The absolute 512 MiB minimum is therefore dominated by the incremental-memory condition. This is a conservative sampled guard limit, not proof that graph allocation cannot jump below it between readings. No measured incremental MiB per graph exists; the nominal increase is 12 main graph slots per GPU plus unchanged draft graphs, with source admission behavior permitting already-recorded keys to capture beyond the nominal count.

## Attribution and required evidence

Current engine/src/volta.rs:3344–3379 keys main graphs by local token rows and context bucket, captures on the second occurrence, and runs a new absent key eagerly when the four-graph cache is full. A 4K prefill can consume early repeated context-bucket slots before decode. Additional admission is therefore mechanically plausible on HP as well as DP. It adds capture and executable metadata costs while leaving weights, KV capacity, tensor scratch, head computation and sampling outside the captured layers unchanged.

Actual matched HP graph-4 receipts establish fixed throughput of 75.100–75.397 tokens/s and mixed throughput of 62.133–62.291 tokens/s. They do not expose graph hit, miss, capture or per-key counts. The earlier instrumented HP trace has graph tracing disabled; its aggregate worker-layer times and step gaps cannot isolate graph-launch savings. The old fixed/mixed gap fractions of approximately 10.95 and 11.21 percent include setup, exchange, logging, CPU assembly and rounding, so attributing them to eager graph execution would be unsupported. The prior DP policy failures also do not prove an HP outcome. There is no measured HP eligible-key occupancy, eager launch fraction or per-key eager-versus-replay cost from which to justify the proposed 5–15 percent range. Better memory headroom alone does not supply that missing attribution.

A confirming experiment would first need a source-bound HP graph trace or equivalent already-reviewed per-key evidence showing frequent eligible decode keys running eagerly and enough removable wall time. A 5 percent throughput increase requires at least T/21 removed wall time: approximately 1.29 seconds in the fixed 27.16–27.27 second arms and 1.57 seconds in the mixed 32.88–32.96 second arms, before new capture costs. Existing actual evidence does not assign that much time to missed graph reuse. No new trace, build or GPU work was authorized for this decision.

## Conditional protocol if revisited

The existing graph-cache-review.md protocol remains the basis for a future screen. Use a fresh graph-16 process with the unchanged V3 binary and exact hp-gpu-01 smoke, warmups, fixed/mixed requests and 2,048 outputs per phase. Include capture costs, bind all configuration/source/model hashes and preserve zero cache reuse. Stop on either phase gaining less than 3 percent, any capture/instantiate or API error, incomplete token work, sampled reserve loss, or deadline risk. One screening arm cannot establish a promoted gain. A promising result still needs fresh graph-16 B2 and graph-4 A2 arms with swapped improvements of at least 5 percent in each phase, original latency limits, and graph-16 teacher qualification before promotion.

The proposed screen must start before 05:53 UTC, and its estimated complete validation chain ends around 06:10 UTC, leaving eight minutes until the unchanged 06:18:26 UTC release. That estimate leaves little recovery margin and is conditional on every run finishing normally. Given the missing measured attribution and the remaining publication work, defer this candidate rather than book a hypothetical gain. This decision does not establish that graph-16 is universally unhelpful.
